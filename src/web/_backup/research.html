§1Tokenizer forensics

Every call reports prompt_bytes and provider-reported input_tokens. The ratio between them — bytes per token — is a fingerprint of the tokenizer. It should not reveal anything interesting. It reveals a lot.

We sent three prompt types at the same byte sizes: song-toads (natural English filler), needle-p50 (same filler with an embedded retrieval code), and random (hex-encoded random bytes). The bytes/token ratio on structured text tells you about vocabulary size and merge quality. The ratio on random bytes tells you about the tokenizer's fallback strategy.

F1All OpenAI models share one tokenizer

gpt-3.5-turbo-0125, gpt-4o-mini, gpt-4.1-mini, gpt-4.1-nano, and gpt-5.4-mini all produce identical bytes/token ratios — 4.29–4.41 on structured text, 1.63–1.66 on random. Five models across at least three generations, all using the same tokenizer.

Tokenizer stability across generations means OpenAI can share KV caches, embedding tables, and prefix-cache infrastructure across model versions without translation layers.

F2All Gemini models share a different tokenizer — one that is nearly byte-level on random input

The three Gemini models produce 1.15 bytes/token on random hex — nearly every byte becomes its own token. This is consistent with a tokenizer that has extensive byte-level fallback paths — likely a SentencePiece variant with byte-fallback enabled.

Gemini's tokenizer inflates random/high-entropy content into far more tokens than OpenAI's does. This has direct cost and context-window implications — see §2.

F3Anthropic's tokenizer is the most efficient on natural language

Claude Haiku 4.5 achieves 3.79–3.90 bytes/token on structured text — roughly 13% fewer tokens than OpenAI for the same English text, and 7% fewer than Gemini. Fewer tokens means less KV cache, fewer attention operations, and lower cost.

§2The real context window

A model's context window is measured in tokens. But the effective context window in bytes depends entirely on what you put in it. Random content hits the wall 2.5–3.8× sooner.

gpt-5.4-mini accepts 1 MB of English but rejects 600 KB of random content. Same model, same API — because 600 KB of hex encodes to ~367k tokens (at 1.64 bpt), which exceeds the context window, while 1 MB of English encodes to ~227k tokens (at 4.40 bpt).

Your context window is not a number on a spec sheet. It is a function of your content's entropy and your tokenizer's fallback strategy.

§4Negative scaling in Gemini Lite

Gemini 3.1 Flash Lite gets faster from 400 KB to 600 KB. The TTFT floor drops from 1,181 ms to 912 ms as prompt size grows 50%. This should not happen.

The simplest explanation is a routing transition near ~100k tokens that shifts the request to a compute tier with better throughput. The flat region from 256–400 KB is the transition zone; by 601 KB the request has cleanly landed on the new tier.

A request at 601 KB completes its entire round trip — prefill and decode of 96 tokens — in 1,148 ms at the floor. A request at 256 KB, with 2.3× fewer tokens, takes 1,704 ms. The longer request finishes first.

F4Prefill fraction tells you what regime a model is in

For Gemini Lite at 1 MB, prefill is 87.8% of total time — decode for 96 tokens costs 240 ms. For gpt-5.4-mini at 1 MB, prefill is only 24.4% — decode for 100 tokens costs 10.4 seconds. Total request: 1.97 s vs 13.7 s.

Every output token at 1 MB context costs ~108 ms on gpt-5.4-mini vs ~3.3 ms on Gemini Lite. The difference between max_tokens=100 and max_tokens=1000 at 1 MB is ~97 seconds on gpt-5.4-mini versus ~3 seconds on Gemini Lite.

§6The universal 50/50 split

When fed random hex with a "write a story" instruction appended, every model that can produce short output produces exactly a 50/50 split between very short (≤10 tokens) and very long (~1,000 tokens). No model produces a medium-length response. Not once.

Seven out of nine models show a perfectly bimodal distribution with zero observations in between. At temperature = 0, the 50/50 split implies the model is at a near-exact tie in the logit for the first token. The per-call nonce pushes the decision one way or the other.

The two exceptions: gpt-4.1-mini and gpt-3.5-turbo always produce long output. The larger model is more willing to engage with random content, not less.

Vary temperature from 0.0 to 1.0 and track the short/long ratio. If the tie is a sharp phase transition, even temperature = 0.1 should push the split decisively one way.

F5No backend drift detected in any sweep

We checked system_fingerprint and provider_model_version within every sweep. No changes were observed. The latency patterns are stable features of a single deployment, not artifacts of mid-sweep routing changes.

§9What this means

Eight concrete takeaways, ordered by how actionable they are.

1Model selection must be context-length-aware

There is no single fastest model. gpt-4.1-nano wins below ~64 KB; gpt-5.4-mini wins at 64–400 KB; gemini-3.1-flash-lite wins above 400 KB. A routing layer that selects model by input size would outperform any single model by 1.5–2.5×.

2OpenAI decode cost makes long-context generation expensive

At 1 MB context, gpt-5.4-mini spends 76% of wall time on decode — 10.4 s for 100 tokens. Gemini Lite spends 12% — 240 ms for 96 tokens.

3Context windows are content-dependent

Random/high-entropy content consumes 2.5–3.8× more tokens per byte. Applications ingesting base64, hex, or compressed data should expect to hit limits much sooner.

4Gemini Lite's long-context route is qualitatively different

Negative prefill scaling, simultaneously dropping decode cost, and a total time that is faster at 601 KB than 256 KB — all three together point to a routing transition near ~100k tokens to a fundamentally different compute environment.

5Tokenizer choice is architecture, not preprocessing

The same tokenizer across 5 OpenAI generations means embedding tables and KV caches are locked to one vocabulary. Anthropic's more efficient tokenizer compounds — fewer tokens means fewer operations and lower cost.

6The compliance boundary is universal

A perfectly bimodal 50/50 output distribution across 7 models and 3 providers suggests a convergent property of instruction-tuned models at the refuse/comply boundary. The boundary is a knife-edge tie that a small perturbation can tip.

7Gemini 2.5 Flash has a long-context output suppression

Unlike its newer siblings, 2.5 Flash voluntarily truncates open-ended output past 256 KB. This was reversed in the 3.x generation.

8Scaling exponents are not fixed properties of a model

Local α varies by 0.5–1.0 across size regimes. Gemini Lite: α = 0.35 at 64–128 KB, α = −0.64 at 400–601 KB. gpt-4.1-nano: α = 0.25 at 32–64 KB, α = 1.64 at 601–801 KB. The interesting signal is where the exponent changes.
